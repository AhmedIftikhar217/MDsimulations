## 1. Draft methods paragraph

> **Molecular dynamics simulations.** Ten copies of the BMP-2 knuckle-epitope
> peptide P24 (KIPKASSVPTELSAISTLYL, residues 73–92 of mature human BMP-2,
> UniProt P12643) were simulated in explicit solvent. Peptides were capped with
> N-terminal acetyl and C-terminal N-methylamide groups to avoid the artificial
> terminal charges that an excised fragment would otherwise carry, and were
> built in an extended conformation (φ = −135°, ψ = 135°) so that no particular
> fold was imposed on the starting state. The ten copies were placed at random
> positions and orientations in a cubic box, subject to a minimum
> centre-of-mass separation of 2.5 nm and no inter-peptide heavy-atom contact
> below 0.5 nm, so that the initial condition was a dispersed monomer solution.
>
> The box edge was set by the target peptide concentration. For N = 10 copies at
> C = 10 mM, V = N/(N_A·C) = 1.6605 × 10³ nm³, giving a cubic edge of 11.84 nm.
> This box was checked against the minimum-image requirement using a worst-case
> solute dimension of 8.24 nm — the diagonal of a hypothetical ribbon of all ten
> peptides fully extended in a single β-sheet — which requires an edge of at
> least 11.24 nm at a 1.0 nm cutoff with 0.5 nm of solvent padding. The
> concentration constraint was therefore the binding one, and the box satisfies
> both.
>
> The system was solvated with OPC water and neutralised with Na⁺/Cl⁻ at a
> background ionic strength of 150 mM, giving approximately 54,400 water
> molecules and 221,000 atoms in total. Protein was described by the Amber
> ff19SB force field and water by OPC, the water model against which ff19SB was
> parameterised. Because OpenMM's solvation routine packs water approximately
> 5% below bulk density, the box was built slightly enlarged so that the
> NPT-equilibrated volume relaxes onto the design volume; the equilibrated
> volume and the resulting concentration are reported in Table S1.
>
> Simulations used OpenMM 8.6. Electrostatics were treated with particle-mesh
> Ewald at a real-space cutoff of 1.0 nm and an Ewald error tolerance of
> 5 × 10⁻⁴; Lennard-Jones interactions used the same cutoff with an isotropic
> long-range dispersion correction. Bonds to hydrogen were constrained and
> water was held rigid. Hydrogen mass repartitioning to 3.0 amu permitted a 4 fs
> time step. Temperature was maintained at 310 K by a Langevin middle
> (BAOAB) integrator with a 1 ps⁻¹ collision frequency, and pressure at 1 bar by
> a Monte Carlo barostat applied every 25 steps.
>
> Each replicate was energy-minimised, heated from 100 K to 310 K over 200 ps
> under NVT with 1000 kJ mol⁻¹ nm⁻² positional restraints on solute heavy atoms,
> then equilibrated for 1.8 ns under NPT while the restraints were released in
> five stages (1000, 500, 100, 10, 0 kJ mol⁻¹ nm⁻²). Production was 10 ns under
> NPT with coordinates saved every 10 ps. Three independent replicates were run
> from different random seeds derived deterministically from a single master
> seed (20260826).
>
> Analysis used MDTraj. Inter-peptide contacts were counted between heavy atoms
> within 0.45 nm under the minimum-image convention; two peptides sharing at
> least five such contacts were treated as associated, and cluster sizes were
> obtained as connected components of the resulting association graph. Cluster
> extents were measured on coordinates unwrapped across periodic boundaries, and
> the largest extent reached was compared with the box edge to confirm that no
> aggregate approached its own periodic image at any point in any trajectory.

## 2. Where each number comes from

### 2.1 Box volume

The box volume *is* the concentration claim — it is the number a reviewer will
check against your experiment, so it is derived, not chosen.

Two constraints must both hold.

**(A) Concentration.**

```
V = N / (N_A · C)
  = 10 / (6.02214076×10²³ mol⁻¹ × 10×10⁻³ mol L⁻¹)
  = 1.6605×10⁻²¹ L
  = 1.6605×10³ nm³                (1 L = 10²⁴ nm³)
```

Cubic edge `L = V^(1/3) = 11.8418 nm`.

**(B) Minimum image.** A solute must never interact with its own periodic
image:

```
L ≥ D_max + 2·(r_cut + buffer)      and      L ≥ 2·r_cut
```

`D_max` is the largest object the solute can become — for an aggregation study,
that is not one peptide. Three bounding scenarios for 10 copies of a 20-mer:

| scenario | construction | D_max |
|---|---|---|
| dispersed | one extended peptide, 20 × 0.335 nm | 6.70 nm |
| globular | all 10 fused into one sphere at protein density | 3.69 nm |
| **sheet** | **10 extended strands side by side, 0.48 nm apart — ribbon diagonal** | **8.24 nm** |

Taking the conservative sheet case, `L ≥ 8.24 + 2·(1.0 + 0.5) = 11.24 nm`.

**Binding constraint: concentration** (11.84 > 11.24 nm), with 0.6 nm to spare.
This is a comfortable place to be — the box is set by the physics you are
reporting, and the periodicity requirement is satisfied as a consequence rather
than by luck.

> **Concentration ceiling.** With 10 copies you cannot go above ~11.7 mM without
> violating minimum image. Higher concentration requires *more copies*, not a
> smaller box. `scripts/00_box_size.py --scan` prints the full trade-off table.

### 2.2 System composition

| quantity | value | how |
|---|---|---|
| peptide mass | 2,174.59 g/mol | residue masses at pH 7.4 + ACE + NME |
| peptide charge | +1 e each, +10 e total | 2 Lys (+2), 1 Glu (−1), neutral caps |
| atoms per peptide | 323 | all-atom, ff19SB |
| peptide excluded volume | 2.64 nm³ | v̄ = 0.73 cm³/g |
| water molecules | ~54,400 | bulk OPC number density 33.2 nm⁻³ |
| Na⁺ / Cl⁻ | ~148 / ~158 | 150 mM background + 10 Cl⁻ neutralising |
| **total atoms** | **~221,000** | OPC is a 4-site model |

### 2.3 Why these settings

| choice | reason |
|---|---|
| **ff19SB + OPC** | ff19SB was parameterised against OPC. Pairing it with TIP3P reintroduces the amino-acid-independent backbone bias ff19SB exists to remove. The pair travels together. |
| **310 K** | 37 °C. Physiological, and the defensible choice for a biomedical paper. |
| **Capped termini** | P24 is an excised fragment. Free NH₃⁺/COO⁻ termini are charges BMP-2 does not have there, and on a 20-mer they would dominate the electrostatics. |
| **Extended start** | Imposing a fold would predetermine the answer. Extended is the neutral prior. |
| **Dispersed start** | If peptides begin in contact, any "aggregation" is the initial condition. |
| **4 fs + HMR** | Hydrogen mass repartitioning to 3.0 amu slows the fastest bonded motions enough for a 4 fs step (Hopkins et al., *JCTC* 2015). Verified: solute H at 3.000 amu, water H unchanged at 1.008 since rigid water must not be repartitioned. Set `hydrogen_mass_amu: 1.008` and `timestep_fs: 2.0` for the conservative alternative. |
| **LangevinMiddle** | BAOAB splitting — the best configurational accuracy per step among OpenMM's Langevin integrators. |
| **Dispersion correction** | Required for a correct density under NPT with a truncated LJ potential. |

## 3. What a 10 ns run of this system can and cannot support

**This matters more than any other section here.** Ten nanoseconds is short
relative to peptide self-assembly, which runs from hundreds of nanoseconds to
milliseconds. State the scope honestly and the work is sound; overclaim and a
reviewer will find it immediately.

**Supported by 10 ns × 3 replicates:**

- Conformational relaxation of the peptide from its extended start — RMSD and
  Rg plateau, transient secondary-structure propensity.
- Whether the peptide is intrinsically disordered under these conditions.
- **Early-stage** encounter events: first contacts, initial dimer formation,
  which residues mediate first approach.
- Solvation structure and system-level QC (density, temperature, energy drift).

**Not supported by 10 ns:**

- Equilibrium aggregation propensity, critical aggregation concentration, or
  any free energy of association. Ten nanoseconds does not sample
  association/dissociation often enough for an equilibrium constant.
- Fibril or β-sheet formation. These take orders of magnitude longer.
- Any claim that peptides *do not* aggregate. Absence of aggregation in 10 ns is
  evidence about the timescale, not about the thermodynamics.

**If the paper's question is self-assembly**, the honest routes are: extend to
0.5–1 µs per replicate (a config change: `production.length_ns`); increase the
replicate count and report the distribution of outcomes; or use enhanced
sampling (replica exchange, metadynamics on an association coordinate). The 10 ns
protocol here is a correct and well-validated *starting point* — treat it as the
pilot that establishes the system is sound, then extend.

**One replicate is not a measurement.** Association is stochastic; a single
trajectory has no error bar. The config runs three replicates by default; five is
better. Report the spread across replicates, not just a mean.

## 4. Reporting checklist

Things reviewers of MD papers ask for, and where to find each:

- [ ] Force field and water model, with versions — `provenance_*.json`
- [ ] Box geometry, volume, and the resulting concentration — `build_summary.json`
- [ ] **Equilibrated** volume and concentration, not just the design values — `equil_summary.json`
- [ ] Ion counts and how neutralisation was handled — `build_summary.json`
- [ ] Integrator, timestep, constraints, HMR — `config` + `provenance_*.json`
- [ ] Thermostat/barostat type and coupling parameters — `config`
- [ ] Equilibration protocol and length — §1 above
- [ ] Production length, frame interval, number of replicates — `prod_summary.json`
- [ ] Software versions and hardware — `provenance_*.json`
- [ ] Random seeds — `build_summary.json` (`seeds`)
- [ ] Evidence the box was large enough — `analysis.json` → `minimum_image`
- [ ] Convergence/QC: temperature, density, energy drift — `analysis.json` → `quality_control`
